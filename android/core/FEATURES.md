# Radon Player

**All your music in one place: on your phone and on your PC.**

Radon Player plays your own music collection *and* the services you already use (Tidal, YouTube Music, Apple Music and Spotify) in one good-looking app. Songs play in FLAC, hi-res where it's available, with a full equalizer and real sound-quality tools. On top of that you get your own listening stats, a yearly Wrapped, a personal DJ, synced lyrics, and tools that tidy up your music files.

The Android app and the Windows PC app are now **one app built from the same code**, so the screens, settings and features match on both. Pair them and your phone becomes a remote for the PC, or pick up on one where you stopped on the other.

## Playing music & Now playing

- **Now playing:** A big cover on a background in the cover's own colours, with the song, artist and album, a ☆ to favorite it and ⋯ for its options. Swipe the cover for the next or previous song, swipe up for the queue, tap it for lyrics, double-tap to pause.
- **Quality badge:** Under the seek bar you see what you're hearing, for example "FLAC 24-bit/96 kHz", the stream's quality, or "Offline". If a song is streaming, tap **⤓ FLAC** to save it as a FLAC file.
- **Lyrics, Output and Queue buttons:** A small pill under the controls switches the cover for the lyrics or the queue. The speaker button opens the Equalizer for the output you're playing through.
- **Mono button:** The headphone icon at the top puts both sides of the music in each ear, which helps when you only have one earbud in. It turns red while mono is on.
- **Up next & queues:** The queue shows what you added ("Queue", which you can clear) and then "Continue Playing" from the album, playlist or folder you started. Drag songs to reorder them, tap one to play it, long-press for its menu.
- **Shuffle, Repeat and Autoplay:** Shuffle, Repeat (all or one) and Autoplay sit above the queue. With Autoplay on, songs like the current one keep playing after the queue runs out.
- **Queues (Settings › Your music):** Gives each folder, album, artist and playlist you play from its own queue, kept where you left it. Go back to any of them, rename them, move them up or down, or remove all the others.
- **Smart shuffle (Settings › Playback):** Shuffle favors songs you love (play a lot) but haven't heard in a while, so it isn't pure chance.
- **Custom EQ… (per song):** Gives a song (or several picked songs) its own EQ on the output you're listening on: its own Headphones correction, Sound preset or bands, and Boost. Whenever that song plays there, its EQ is used instead of the usual one; on other outputs, and for every other song, the usual EQ stays. Now playing shows **C EQ** on a song that has one, red while it's in effect (tap it to change it), and the Equalizer button greys out while it is. Bass and treble, dither and the other sound settings stay as they are.
- **Volume… (per song):** Makes a song louder or quieter, from −12 to +12 dB, and remembers it, for the output you're listening on (your headphones, the car, the speaker: each one the app tells apart, as the Equalizer does) or for every output. So a song that's quiet on your headphones can get louder there without blasting in the car. Works on one song or many picked ones. Now playing shows a speaker with **!** on a song that has a volume change, red while it's in effect on this output (tap it to change it). A song's own volume always applies whenever you've set one; songs without one play untouched. Handy for quiet unreleased tracks.
- **Speed… (per song):** Plays a song at half speed up to twice its speed. You can let the pitch move with it, for that "sped-up edit" sound.
- **Now playing controls:** Back, Play and Next sit together in the middle; the speaker on the left shows the volume bar, and the button on the right opens the visualizer settings in the cover's place. **C EQ** at the top sets up the playing song's own EQ (red while it's in use).
- **Shuffle and Repeat beside the controls:** While the big cover shows on Now playing, Shuffle sits left of Back and Repeat right of Next, lit when on.
- **Custom volume button:** **C** and a speaker at the top of Now playing open the playing song's own volume straight away (red while it's in use).

## Your library

Open the ☰ menu to find everything. Pages can be shown as a list, a grid or a compact grid, and each page has its own search box that looks only through that page.

- **Pinned:** Pin an album, artist, playlist or folder to keep it at the top of the menu, newest first. Long-press a pin to unpin it or move it up.
- **My music:** Every song in your folders plus your downloads, with Play, Shuffle and **DJ** buttons. Sort by Title, Artist, Album or Added (newest first). Sorting by artist or album groups the songs under headings.
- **Albums, EPs and Singles:** Your songs grouped by release, shown as cover grids. Each release is in exactly one of the three, by its album tag, as taggers name them: an album ending in "- EP" is an EP, one ending in "- Single" (or "(Single)") is a single, and every other album is an album, however few of its songs you have (those are under **Low song count albums**). Songs with no album at all are singles. Albums has two buttons, **Albums** (more than six songs) and **Low song count albums** (six or fewer), to keep the big ones uncluttered. **Select** lets you tick several albums and use Options on all their songs (Genres, Folders, Artists and Playlists have Select too; picked playlists can also be deleted, renamed, opened or favorited). While you pick, the bar with Options, Clear and Cancel stays at the bottom of the screen, so you can stop picking wherever you've scrolled to (Back stops it too, and so does going to another tab). Each tab also has a **Collage** of your top releases,.
- **Your copy first on artist pages:** An artist's top songs and song grid use your own file (or download) of a song whenever you have one, so it plays your copy and shows its cover; the streaming version only when you don't have the song.
- **Covers for every song:** Songs you don't have that came without a picture (ones only your history knows, demos) get their cover looked up on your streaming service, Deezer, Last.fm or iTunes; if none has one, the artist's picture shows instead of a blank note. A picture from one of your files only ever appears on that artist's songs.
- **Covers kept on your device:** Every cover and artist picture is saved after it first loads (up to 512 MB), and the cover found for each song is remembered, so nothing is downloaded or looked up twice.
- **Missing from this album or EP:** On one of your albums or EPs, the songs of it you don't have (from the album's tracklist) show at the bottom under **Missing from this album**, with **Download missing tracks…** to pick which to download. Songs you have under a slightly different name (a version, "feat.", punctuation) or at the same length count as yours, not missing.
- **Artists:** The artists you listen to most, ranked by every play counted together (see Stats). An artist's page has Play, Shuffle, **Change picture…** (pictures from Google, Genius and Wikipedia, or one of your own), **Discovery playlist** and **Merge into…**. Your songs by them show as a grid (or a list), sorted by **Most played** (the default), **Least played**, **Title**, **Album** or **Newest**.
- **Artists › View:** A **View** menu at the top of Artists switches between **Most plays** (your artists, most played first, with their pictures) and **Folders**: just your artist folders (the ones inside an Artists folder, not Singles and EPs or any other), shown as folders with no pictures. Tap one for every song in it; Select and Sort work there too.
- **Artist page source:** A dropdown at the top of an artist's page: **Everything** (streaming and yours) or **Only my music** (downloaded and local), remembered. Everything uses your own copy wherever you have one, and an album you fully have opens as your own album, with your tags and covers.
- **Create artist playlist:** Long-press (right-click on the PC) an artist, or use the button on their page: a playlist named after them with every song of theirs you have (features too), by album and track. Doing it again updates it. **Artist playlists include songs you don't have** (Settings › Your music, off by default) adds their streaming songs too.
- **Search all albums:** Searching the Albums tab looks through every album, big and small; the Albums / Low song count split returns when the search is empty.
- **Genres:** Your songs grouped by genre.
- **Moods in smart playlists:** Each rule in the smart playlist editor has a **Moods ▾** dropdown beside Genre and contains: pick a mood and the playlist keeps only songs in it (Any mood takes it off).
- **Mood lists:** ☰ › Mood lists, after Smart playlists: a playlist for each mood (Happy, Energetic, Party, Hype, Chill, Calm, Dreamy, Focus, Romantic, Sad, Dark, Intense) with your songs in that mood. They're made the first time you open the tab and then stay as they are until you tap **Refresh** (on the list or in its long-press menu), which picks up new songs and says what changed ("Chill: 12 new songs, 3 gone"). **Refresh all** does every list at once, and each shows when it was last refreshed. List or grid, search, Select, sorting, Play, Shuffle, DJ, Pin and Export work as for playlists.
- **Playlists:** Make playlists, rename them, sort their songs (Order, Title, Artist, Album, Most played, Length), **Export** them as .m3u8, and **Import a playlist (.m3u8)**. Put playlists into **folders**, including your services' playlists.
- **Smart playlists:** ☰ › Smart playlists, below Playlists: playlists that fill themselves from your music by rules and keep up as it changes (or stay fixed until you tap Refresh). Rules can use genre, mood, artist, album, title, release type (album, EP, single), format (FLAC 24, FLAC 16, MP3…), downloaded or your own files, favorite or not, play count, last played, never played, recently added, skipped a lot or never skipped, length and folder. Combine them with All or Any, pick the order (random, most played, recently added, title…) and how many songs; the editor shows how many fit as you go. One tap adds ready-made ones: Most played I haven't heard in a month, Never played, Recently added, Chill evening, Workout, Favorites I skip, Forgotten favorites, Hi-res, Heavy rotation, Deep cuts, Party, Focus and more. Play, Shuffle, DJ from them, Edit, Rename, Delete, Export, Pin, folders, Select, sorting and layouts work as for playlists, and backups keep them.
- **Folders:** Browse your songs by the folders they're stored in, each one shown as a folder (in the list and in the grid, on the phone and the PC), with its location and song count.
- **Format on every song:** Each song of yours shows its file's kind at the end of its row: FLAC and WAV with their bit depth ("FLAC 24", "WAV 16"), and MP3, M4A, AAC, Ogg and the rest by name. Songs downloaded from a service also show a ⤓ download mark, so you can tell them from the files you already had.
- **Downloads:** A list in the ☰ menu of the songs you downloaded from a service (Tidal, or YouTube where Tidal hasn't got them), the newest first, each with its quality, as a list or a grid.
- **YouTube downloads at their best:** When a song comes from YouTube, it's YouTube's highest quality: Opus at about 160 kbps, or AAC 256 kbps where YouTube offers it, saved as it comes (an .opus or .m4a file with its tags and cover), and labelled honestly. YouTube has no lossless audio, so a FLAC made from it would only be bigger.
- **24-bit FLACs and 32-bit FLACs:** Two lists in the ☰ menu with only your FLAC songs of that bit depth, each showing its sample rate ("24-bit · 96 kHz"), the highest sample rate first. They work as a list or a grid. The app reads each file's format once and remembers it, so only new songs are read again.
- **Favorites and Not favorited:** All the songs you've starred, and the ones you haven't.
- **Recently played:** Your latest songs, with a Refresh button.
- **Skips:** Which songs you skip most or least, or hear all the way through most, for this week, month, year or all time.
- **Your service's library:** When you browse a service instead of your own music, its library (favorites, playlists) gets its own entry in the menu.
- **Sort every list:** Each tab has a **Sort** menu with the orders that make sense for it, and remembers the one you pick. Songs (My music, Favorites, Not favorited, Recently played, Downloads, 24-bit and 32-bit FLACs, a genre's or a folder's songs): Title A–Z or Z–A, Artist A–Z or Z–A, Album, Recently added, Oldest added, Most played, Least played, Recently played, Not played in a while, Longest, Shortest and Shuffled (the FLAC tabs also by sample rate). Albums, EPs and Singles: Name A–Z or Z–A, Artist, Recently added, Oldest added, Most and Least played, Recently played, Not played in a while, Most and Fewest songs, Longest, Shortest and Shuffled. Artists: Most played (as before), Least played, Name, Recently played, Recently added, Most songs, Longest and more. Genres and Folders: the same as albums, less Artist. Playlists: Your order, Name, Newest, Oldest, Recently changed, Most or Fewest songs, Longest and Shortest. Sorts by plays use your stats (Last.fm, Spotify and the app's own).
- **Which tab a song is in:** Search results show next to each of your own songs which tab of My music it's in: **In Albums**, **In EPs** or **In Singles**.
- **Voice typing:** Every search bar has a microphone button: say what you want instead of typing it. The main search, each page's own search, Ask the DJ, the lyrics and cover searches, and Choose yourself in the scans. The phone uses its speech recognizer, the PC uses Windows' dictation.
- **Options for selected songs:** Long-press songs to pick them, then: Play, Shuffle play, Play after the current song, Add to the queue, Add to a playlist, Add to or Remove from favorites, Download, Tagger…, Volume…, Speed…, Upgrade to FLAC…, Go to album, Discovery playlist for (the artist), File location, Copy the names, Share and Delete permanently…. The options sheet always opens all the way, so every option shows. A checkbox decides whether the selection closes after each option or stays open so you can use several.
- **One look for song options:** Long-pressing a song, right-clicking it on the PC, or its ⋮ button opens the same options sheet as for selected songs, with that song's extras (Go to the artist, Remove download, Select…).
- **Your own copy first:** Lists made from your plays (Skips, Recently played) show your own file of each song, with its cover, and the Tagger edits it, rather than the streamed copy the play was counted on.
- **Your copy first:** Wherever a song plays or is listed (playlists with streaming songs, search results, a service's albums, artist pages, radios, Recently played, Favorites, Most played, Wrapped, smart and mood lists, Up next), if you have it yourself as a file or a download, your copy plays and its cover, tags and format show, never the stream. A live or remixed version is never swapped for your studio one, and a queue saved before you had a song plays your copy once you have it.
- **Nothing is deleted without asking:** Anything that deletes or removes something (songs, downloads, playlists, folders, queues, presets, songs from a playlist) asks "Are you sure?" first, and only does it when you say yes.
- **Tidy folders after deleting:** When you delete songs, remove downloads, replace or upgrade copies, or move files with Fix albums and artists, the folders they were in go too once no music at all is left in them (only a cover, lyrics and the like): up to the artist's folder, never your music folder itself or Folders to leave alone. On the PC they go to the Recycle Bin; on the phone only completely empty folders in the app's download folder can be removed.

## Moods

Every song gets a mood from how it sounds and, if you like, from its words. ☰ › Moods shows them.

- **Moods:** Every song of yours (files and downloads) gets a mood (Happy, Energetic, Party, Hype, Chill, Calm, Dreamy, Focus, Romantic, Sad, Dark or Intense) and up to two more. ☰ › Moods shows them as coloured tiles with song counts; tap one for its songs.
- **How moods are found:** The app listens to half a minute from the middle of each song, on your device: how loud and energetic it is, its tempo, how bright or warm it sounds, how busy it is, and roughly whether it's major or minor. It adds what the song's genre suggests and, with a Last.fm key, the tags people gave the song and its artist. Tags count most, then genre; the sound tips the balance.
- **Moods scans:** The Moods tab has two scans. **Scan moods (1 minute per song)** hears a minute from the middle of each song and runs by itself after your music is scanned (on the phone, while charging). **Full scan (whole songs)** hears every song from start to end, including how its energy changes and its different parts, so the moods are surer; a whole-song result replaces a 1-minute one. Both show how far they've got, the song being heard and about how long is left, with Stop; Full scan carries on where it stopped.
- **Moods in your files:** "Write moods into the files" puts each song's mood in its file's MOOD tag (FLAC, Ogg and Opus; MP3, WAV and AIFF; M4A). It's on once a Full scan has finished, and the app asks before writing many files. On the PC it covers all your files; on the phone only the songs the app downloaded (the rest keep their moods in the app). A MOOD tag already in a file is used as that song's mood, so retagged or moved files keep theirs. The Moods tab shows how many songs already have their mood in the file and how many are still to write (and, while writing, how far it's got). Songs whose file already has a MOOD tag are skipped by both scans, so a scan only hears the songs without one.
- **Wait for the writing:** The Moods tab asks you to keep the app open until the moods are done writing. The scans and the writing take a long time the first time, but once the moods are in your files you never need to do it again. A message pops up at the bottom of the screen when the writing is done.
- **Several moods for a song:** A song can be in more than one mood (it shows in each mood's list). In the Tagger's Mood row, tick every mood that fits; each tick is saved straight away, into the file's MOOD tag too ("Happy;Party"), and Clear has them worked out again.
- **Tagger mood:** The Tagger's Tags tab has a Mood row: pick a mood for the songs, or Clear to have it worked out again. Saved in the app and, where the file can be written, in the file.
- **Moods tab rows:** Each mood's row shows six of its songs (different artists first), laid along the row up to the song count.
- **Fix a wrong mood:** The Moods tab notes that moods won't always be spot on (scanning both the sound and the lyrics gives the best results, about 90% right) and reminds you: long-press a song (right-click on the PC) › Tagger… › Tags › Mood, the row under Track. It's saved straight away and written into the file where the app can.
- **Moods in backups:** Backups include every song's moods. On restore, on the phone or the PC, they're matched to your songs by title and artist (length within a few seconds, album to break ties), so you never need to scan again.
- **Every mood that fits:** Each song gets its own score for all 12 moods and is in every mood that fits it (usually 1 to 4), not one mood and two extras. Its best fit is its main mood (used for Wrapped's shares and the MOOD tag). Mood lists, smart playlists, the DJ and Most played › Moods use them all; in Most played a play counts in each of the song's moods. Existing scans give the new results straight away, no song heard again.
- **Smarter lyrics reading:** The on-device reader reads whole lines and sayings, not single words: "wash my blues away" is relief, not sadness; "not" reaches only the next few words; choruses count more; "used to… but now" and stories about someone else are understood. Songs read by the old reader are read again the next time you tap Read moods from lyrics.
- **Sound and words weighed together:** A clearly upbeat song isn't made sad by a hint of sad words (and a slow, minor one isn't made happy by a hint of glad ones); plainly sad words on an upbeat song still give both moods. When sound and words agree, the mood is surer.
- **AI reading by default:** With an Anthropic key saved, Claude reads the lyrics, scoring every mood and judging what the singer means (irony, sayings, a sad story told cheerfully). The Moods tab offers once to re-read songs read on the device; a song the AI has read is never read again. **Test the key** checks the key and your credits, and a failed request says why in red under the key.
- **Learns from your fixes:** Moods you tick in the Tagger nudge the songs that sound most like that song, and the same artist's other songs, a little the same way (capped; never over a song's own clear moods or your other picks).
- **Check these:** The Moods tab lists songs whose sound and words disagree, or that it isn't sure about: tick the right moods and Save, open the Tagger, or tap Looks right to take it off the list for good.
- **Mood and lyrics writes change nothing else:** Writing moods into files, reading moods from lyrics and the lyrics scanner change only the mood, MOOD_LYRICS and lyrics tags. Title, artist, album, album artist (what Fix albums and artists set), covers and every other tag stay exactly as they were. Files whose tags can't be read whole are left untouched, and an old MP3 or WAV that gets its first full tag keeps its title, artist and album.
- **Sound and words both count:** Every mood weighs both how a song sounds and what its words say: the words weigh more for Sad, Romantic and Dark, the sound more for Energetic, Party, Chill and Focus, and neither ever counts for nothing.
- **Moods already in your files:** When the Moods tab opens and after your music is scanned, the app quickly reads each new file's own MOOD tag (no listening). Moods written on the PC count straight away on the phone after you copy the music over: the tab says "All 3,275 songs already have their moods in their files — no scan needed", or how many still need one, and the scans only listen to those.
- **Most played covers from your files:** Songs time, Albums time and Albums show the cover from your own copy of the song or album first (its file's cover, or the folder's cover.jpg); an online picture only when you don't have it, saved after the first load. Songs time and Albums time open much faster.
- **Mood plays per song:** In Most played › Moods, tap a mood to see its songs, each with how many times you played it in the chosen week, month, year or all time, most played first.
- **Moods in Most played:** A **Moods** view next to Artists time, for this week, month, year or all time: each mood's plays (and about how long), most played first. It's counted from all your play history (Last.fm, Spotify, Apple Music, Deezer and other imports, plus plays here) matched to your own songs, so past years count too, with no scan. Tap a mood for its most played songs.
- **Lyrics scanner:** Finds every song's lyrics and writes them into the file (timed lines when there are some): on the PC all your files, on the phone the songs the app downloaded; other songs and streamed songs in your playlists and favorites keep them in the app. Songs that already have lyrics are skipped. Find it on the Moods tab or Settings › Scanner › Lyrics & moods.
- **Songs with no lyrics found:** Listed after the scan, like the other scans' review screens. For each: Leave alone (remembered), Instrumental, Enter lyrics (typed in, saved into the file or the app), Search the web (opens your browser) or Search again (tries the lyrics sites with a simpler title).
- **Read moods from lyrics:** A second mood scan on the Moods tab reads each song's words for their mood, tone and themes, and counts it together with the sound: the words count more for Sad, Romantic and Dark, the sound more for Energetic, Party, Chill and Focus; instrumentals use the sound alone. It runs on your device for free, understanding "not", "never" and the like. The result goes into the file's MOOD_LYRICS tag (or the app) and backups, so no song is read twice.
- **AI reading of lyrics (optional):** Paste your own Anthropic API key in Settings › Scanner › Lyrics & moods and Claude reads the words for a closer reading (sarcasm, a sad story with happy words). Each song's words go to Anthropic and cost a little per song on your Anthropic account; the key stays on this device, out of backups and sync. Turning it on shows step-by-step how to get a key (sign up at platform.claude.com, add a few dollars of credits under Billing, Create key under API keys, copy it, paste it and Save).
- **Moods everywhere:** Moods from the sound, the files, the lyrics and the Tagger feed Wrapped's mood pages, smart playlist rules, the DJ's mood sets and facts, and Most played.

## Search & the services

Search looks in Tidal, YouTube Music, Deezer, Apple Music, Spotify or **Your music**: pick which from the search bar's menu. Paste a link shared from another app and Radon Player searches for it on the service it came from. Results come as you type, the best matches for what you typed first, and going back to something you searched a moment ago shows it at once. You can search again at any time, even while songs are downloading; while a new search is on its way, the last results stay with a note at the top saying what's being searched.

- **Tidal (Settings › Accounts):** Connect your Tidal subscription and songs play in FLAC, hi-res where Tidal has it. Tidal only falls back to lossy where it has no FLAC. Tidal's favorites, playlists, search and radio work as a library too.
- **Downloads:** Download any song, from any service: it comes from Tidal (it needs a Tidal subscription), or from YouTube when Tidal hasn't got it or isn't connected. Downloads go to the folder you choose (one of your music folders, or a new folder in Music/), sorted with each album right in its artist's folder (Artist / Album), and EPs and singles in Artist / EPs and Artist / Singles, with full tags. Singles and EPs are named as taggers name them ("Title - Single", "Title - EP"), in their folder and in their album tag, so they land in the right tab.
- **What each service gives you (top of Settings › Accounts):** A note explains that Radon Player works best with a Tidal subscription and your own song files, since Tidal is the only way to stream or download in FLAC. Signing in to Spotify, Apple Music, Deezer or YouTube Music is purely for bringing over your data (library, playlists, favorites and listening info); their songs play from Tidal, or from YouTube at lower quality without it. It also explains how to switch to Tidal safely: import your playlists and listening history from your old service first, so nothing is lost, and mentions DumpMedia for turning songs from those services into files you can play as your own.
- **Stream quality (Settings › Your music):** Pick **FLAC 24-bit** (hi-res where Tidal has it), **FLAC 16-bit** (CD quality) or **320 kbps** (Tidal's AAC, as good as MP3 320 and lightest on data) for songs that aren't on your device. It covers every service's songs, since Spotify's, Apple Music's, Deezer's and YouTube Music's all play from Tidal when it's connected. Without Tidal, or for songs Tidal hasn't got, they stream from YouTube at its best (about 128 to 160 kbps).
- **Download quality (Settings › Your music):** Pick **FLAC 24-bit** (hi-res where Tidal has it), **FLAC 16-bit** (CD quality) or **MP3 320 kbps**, and every download comes in it. On the PC the MP3 is made from Tidal's FLAC; on the phone, which has no MP3 maker, it's Tidal's own 320 kbps (AAC). Without a Tidal subscription, downloads come from YouTube at its best (about 128 to 160 kbps; YouTube has no FLAC), whatever is picked.
- **Songs from Spotify, Apple Music and others (Settings › Your music):** They can't be downloaded in the app, so a note under Download quality points to **DumpMedia**, a paid computer app (monthly, or a one-time lifetime licence) that saves songs from those services as files, FLAC included, with your existing subscription. Put the files in one of your music folders and they play as your own songs.
- **Import playlists from the services:** In Tidal, YouTube Music, Deezer, Apple Music or Spotify's library, long-press (right-click on the PC) a playlist and pick **Import to my playlists**, or use **Import all into my playlists** at the top. Each becomes one of your playlists here, its songs streaming until you download them. Importing again adds just the songs added there since.
- **Download a whole playlist:** Long-press (right-click) one of your playlists and pick **Download**, or select several and pick Download. Only the streaming songs that aren't on this device yet are downloaded: your own song files and songs downloaded already are skipped.
- **YouTube Music:** Sign in to browse your YouTube Music library and playlists. Its songs play from Tidal in FLAC. When Tidal doesn't have a song, YouTube's own audio fills the gap.
- **Apple Music:** Sign in to browse your Apple Music library, playlists and favorites. Search works even when you aren't signed in. Songs play from their Tidal match in FLAC.
- **Deezer:** Sign in to browse your Deezer favorites and playlists, like songs, and search Deezer, its artists and albums. Deezer's own audio is locked to Deezer's apps, so its songs play and download from Tidal (in FLAC) or YouTube, like Spotify's and Apple Music's. Coming from Deezer, your whole library comes with you.
- **Spotify:** Connect through your own free Spotify developer app (the account that owns it needs Premium). Settings walks you through it with a **Make an app** button. You get your liked songs, playlists, search and artists' albums, and the songs play from Tidal in FLAC.
- **Signing in on the PC:** YouTube Music, Apple Music and Deezer sign in in your browser; you then paste the cookie the app needs (it shows you where to find it, step by step). It stays on the PC and Sign out forgets it.
- **Sync switches:** Each connected service has a Sync switch that decides whether its library is synced into the app.
- **Copies (Settings › Your music):** Decides what happens when you download a song you already have: **Ask**, **Keep both** or **Replace**. When it asks, you see the copies you have and can choose Download and replace, Download and keep both, or Only the new ones.

## Lyrics

- **Lyrics mode (Settings › Your music, or the lyrics screen):** Lyrics appear on Now playing in place of the cover. **Genius** shows the lyrics as written. **Synced** shows lines timed to the music, lighting up as they're sung. Tap a line to jump to that point in the song.
- **Genius notes:** With Genius lyrics, marked lines have Genius annotations. Tap one to read the best note, with a link to more on Genius.
- **Lyrics search:** If Genius, Tidal and LRCLIB don't find a song, search again by a different artist or title. Lyrics that are found are kept for offline use.
- **Lyrics card:** Tick up to 15 lines and Radon Player makes a picture with them in big type on the cover's colour, with the cover, title and artist. Save it to your device, or share it to Instagram, Facebook, Messages and more.
- **Lyrics into files:** Once a song has finished playing, the lyrics found for it are written into the file's tags (FLAC and MP3), so they stay with the song. On the phone, your own files need All files access (tap **Allow**). Without it, lyrics go only into the app's own downloads.

## Sound

### The Equalizer

Open it from the ☰ menu or from the speaker button on Now playing.

- **Per-output EQ:** Each output (built-in speaker, wired headphones, Bluetooth earbuds, USB DAC, your car) has its own equalizer, which switches in automatically when the sound goes there. Pick an output to edit it even when you aren't using it. Turn **A separate equalizer for each output** off to use one EQ everywhere.
- **Bands:** Choose 3, 5, 10, 15 or 31 bands, each ±12 dB. Tap a band's number to reset it to zero. **Compact** fits every band on one screen.
- **Presets ("Sound"):** Flat, Bass boost, Warm, Vocal, Bright, V-shape, **Chevy Colorado 2023+ (standard)** and **Chevy Colorado 2023+ (Bose)**, tuned for that truck's stock and Bose systems, one for each built-in device (**Xenns Mangird Top Pro**, **Sony WH-1000XM6 · Bluetooth**, **Sony WH-1000XM6 · cable**, **Sony WF-1000XM6 earbuds**, **Galaxy S26 Ultra speaker**: an all-genre shape on top of its Headphone correction), plus Custom.
- **Headphones correction:** Built-in corrections tuned to the Harman target for the Xenns Mangird Top Pro (two measurements), Sony WH-1000XM6 (Bluetooth and cable), Sony WF-1000XM6 earbuds and the Galaxy S26 Ultra's own speakers (approximations).
- **Auto EQ:** Type your headphones' name to find their correction in AutoEq's database, tap **Detect** to look up the ones connected now, or **Import** a correction file. It's added to your list and comes back whenever those headphones connect.
- **Collect all sources:** AutoEq has measurements of the same headphones from several reviewers (oratory1990, Rtings, crinacle, Super Review…). **Collect all sources** adds every one for the headphones connected (or the ones typed) so you can compare them, and new headphones get theirs collected by themselves when they first connect. **Export** saves the correction in use as a ParametricEQ file (AutoEq's and Equalizer APO's format).
- **Your own Sound presets:** **Save as preset…** keeps the bands as they are now as a preset of yours; **Import** reads a GraphicEQ or parametric file (Wavelet, Equalizer APO, AutoEq, Squiglink) as one; **Export** saves the Sound as a GraphicEQ file; a preset of yours can be deleted. Each new output (headphones, earbuds, a speaker) also gets a Sound preset named after it to start from. Your presets are in your backups.
- **Bass and treble:** Two extra ±12 dB tone controls on top of the bands.
- **Boost fader:** Makes everything up to 12 dB louder on this output only, for quiet headphones. A **red line** marks how far you can boost cleanly. Below it the sound is clean; past it, a limiter squashes the peaks so they don't distort, which is fine for noisy places. **Off** returns to normal volume.
- **Pre-gain:** When the EQ boosts something, the app lowers the level automatically so the boosts don't clip.
- **Headphone crossfeed:** Feeds a little of each side into the other, like speakers across a room. It's easier on the ears with old hard-panned records. Choose Light, Medium or Strong.
- **Song crossfade:** Each song fades into the next, over 1 to 12 seconds, with no cut or gap before the fade (not in bit-perfect).
- **Smart transitions:** Turns crossfade off between songs from the same album, so albums that flow from track to track (live albums, mixes) stay seamless.

### Sound quality (Settings › Sound quality)

- **Bit-perfect:** Sends each song to your DAC exactly as it is, at its own sample rate, with no EQ, song volume or speed changes. On the phone it needs Android 14 and a USB DAC, and it only takes over while the DAC is the output: on the speaker, Bluetooth or in the car the EQ keeps working. On the PC it uses exclusive mode, so no other sound plays at the same time. Closing the window (to the tray), quitting, or unplugging a sound device (a DAC, deck or headphones) turns it off, so the PC's other sound comes back.
- **Clip guard:** If a song's volume or the EQ would push a peak past full scale, a limiter catches it so it doesn't crackle. It only touches peaks that would clip.
- **Smooth distortion on loud songs:** Some songs are mastered so loud their peaks are cut off and sound grainy. On the phone it rebuilds those peaks as smooth curves and leaves 3 dB of room so EQ boosts, song volume and resampling never clip them again; on the PC it leaves the same room with a limiter for anything left. Music plays 3 dB quieter while it's on. Off by default, never in bit-perfect.
- **Dither:** Adds a whisper of shaped noise when 16-bit music goes through the EQ, so quiet fades stay smooth instead of grainy. Hi-res music is always dithered as it comes down to 16-bit; only bit-perfect to a USB DAC keeps every bit.
- **High-quality resampling:** When a song's sample rate doesn't match the sound card's or the phone's (44.1 kHz music on a 48 kHz card), it's converted with a longer, sharper filter: cleaner highs. Music already at the right rate, and bit-perfect, are left untouched. On the phone it works on CD-quality music, from the next song.
- **Loudness compensation:** At low volume our ears lose bass and treble first. This lifts them back by as much as the volume is down, and does nothing at full volume.
- **Detail:** Brings forward background sounds, bass lines and small details: the stereo gets a touch wider, presence and air lift a little, and the level drops to match so nothing clips.
- **Mono:** The Now playing button that puts both channels in each ear.

### Bluetooth & outputs

- **Auto mono (phone only):** Turns mono on automatically while the Bluetooth earbuds you pick are connected (for one earbud in), and off when they disconnect.
- **Ignore Bluetooth devices:** Pick Bluetooth devices (like your car) from the ones you've connected before. While it's on, the music stays on the headphones you were using when one of them connects; the device still connects for calls and Android Auto's maps. Turn it on or off in Settings › Playback or with the Bluetooth-with-an-X button on Now playing (long-press or right-click it to choose devices). Phones decide how Bluetooth works, so it may not work on every phone (it needs the phone to keep both connected), and Android Auto's own sound can't be moved. On the PC the music stays on the previous output when Windows switches to an ignored device.

## Stats & Wrapped

### How your plays are counted

Radon Player adds up every play from wherever you listened:

- **Last.fm (Settings › Last.fm & your stats):** Connect your Last.fm account and everything you play is scrobbled. All your scrobbles, from all time, are the base of your stats. Scrobbles made while offline wait and send later. You need a free Last.fm API account; Settings has a button to make one. Without Last.fm, the plays counted in the app are used instead.
- **Spotify history:** Import the zip from Spotify's privacy page to bring back years of listening. With Spotify connected, your latest plays also come in on their own (Sync gets them now). A play counts from 30 seconds, as Spotify counts streams. Podcasts and videos are left out. Plays of your own local files come in too when Spotify kept their names (in the play itself, or in its spotify:local: address); Spotify leaves many of them out of the zip entirely.
- **Apple Music history:** Import the zip from Apple's privacy page (Get a copy of your data › Apple Media Services), or a library exported from the Music app on a computer (File › Library › Export Library…).
- **Deezer history:** Import the .xlsx listening history from Deezer's privacy settings (My account › Privacy settings › My personal data).
- **YouTube Music history:** Import your history from Google Takeout (takeout.google.com: YouTube and YouTube Music, history as JSON). The steps are shown next to Import. Only songs played in YouTube Music count, not videos.
- **Tidal history:** Import the streaming history from the copy of your data Tidal sends when you ask for it (a privacy request), as the zip or the CSV or JSON file in it.

Imported plays are **added alongside** your Last.fm scrobbles, not swapped for them, and importing the same file again never adds a play twice. **Don't import an app's data if you used Last.fm with that app** (for instance Spotify with Last.fm scrobbling on): its plays are on Last.fm already and would count twice. A note at the top of Settings › Last.fm & your stats explains this. Each history has a **Clear** button, and **Reset plays** at the bottom of that section clears every imported play at once (Last.fm's scrobbles stay), so you can import again the right way. The Spotify import is called **Spotify history**. Songs you play in the app are never counted twice either: while Last.fm is connected, your stats use only its scrobbles, and the app's own play count is just a fallback for when Last.fm isn't connected.

### Your stats

- **Most played:** Your top **Songs**, **Albums** or **Artists** for **This week, This month, This year or All time**. A Refresh line shows how many songs are counted and which sources they came from. **Only songs on this phone/PC** limits the list to music you have.
- **Listening time:** **Songs time**, **Albums time** and **Artists time** show how many hours you listened in a period, charts by hour of the day and day of the week, and your songs, albums or artists ranked by time spent.
- **Time choice (Settings › Last.fm & your stats):** Shows listening time in Songs time, Albums time and Artists time in **Hours** (and minutes) or all in **Minutes**.
- **Milestones:** Songs and artists that passed 100, 250, 500, 1,000… plays in the last month.
- **Repeat:** What you've had on repeat: your top 50 of the last week, top 100 of the month and top 200 of the year.
- **Joint artists:** A duet's plays go to its main artist (Quinn XCII & Jeremy Zucker counts for Quinn XCII). When a pair isn't clear, you choose whose plays they are.
- **Similar artists:** Finds artists that look like the same one written two ways ("cavetown offline" and "Cavetown") and lets you merge them, plays and all, or keep them apart.
- **Duplicates in Most played:** Finds songs counted twice under different names ("N.P.D" and "NPD"). You pick which to merge or leave alone. Versions marked in brackets or after a dash, like "(Stripped)", "(Acoustic Sessions) (Live)", "[Remix]", "- Orchestra" or "(Live)", are songs of their own and are left out; a plain "Live" in a song's name isn't a mark.
- **Merge into… (artist page):** From now on, count one artist's plays as another's.

### Wrapped

- **Your Wrapped:** Your week, month or year in music, or all time, as full-screen slides. Tap the right side for the next slide and the left to go back. Slides include your top songs, artists, album and genre, minutes listened, month by month, when you listen, your best day, streaks, milestones, new favorites, most skipped, never skipped, how you listen (a "finisher" or "picky"), year by year, and a final summary card. A skip means leaving a song with more than 15 seconds left.
- **Styles:** Classic, Neon, Minimal, Paper, Mono and single-colour neon styles.
- **Share a slide:** **Save** (at the top of Wrapped) saves the slide showing as a picture on your device, or Share posts it straight to Instagram or Facebook.
- **Save pictures:** Every Wrapped slide, the summary card, collages, Most played share cards and lyrics cards have a Save button that saves the picture as a PNG on the device: on the phone to your Gallery (Pictures › Radon Player), on the PC to Downloads › Radon Player, opened in its folder so you can see it.
- **Collage:** Your top songs, albums, EPs, singles or artists as a square grid of covers, from 5×5 up to 25×25, with names and play counts. Save it or share it.
- **Share card:** In Most played, your top 15 songs, albums or artists (or by listening time) on one wide card like Wrapped's summary: each with its cover, artist and plays or time, in two columns, shown whole on screen to share or save.
- **Wrapped summary card:** The last slide sums it up on one card to share or save: your top 15 artists and top 15 songs (each with its artist) side by side, your top 5 genres, and your listening time in minutes and in hours.
- **Top 15:** Every top list in Wrapped (songs, artists, albums, genres, singles, EPs…) shows your top 15.
- **Genres and moods:** Your top genres (from your files' genre tags) and your top moods, each with a variety score.
- **Singles and EPs:** Your most played singles and EPs, as your Singles and EPs tabs sort them.
- **Discoveries and moments:** Songs and artists you played for the very first time, the song you replayed most in one day, and each month's top artist.
- **Listening diary:** From 3.0 on, the app notes how long you listen, which playlist, album or search a song came from, whether it was shuffled, and which output it played on (car, earbuds, speaker, PC). Wrapped uses it for **Playlists** (time in each, shuffled vs in order), **Devices** (time per output), **Most searched** (only songs you search for often: started from search 4 or more times within a week) and your longest session.
- **Listener type:** A fun name and a line about how you listen: mainstream or underground (from Deezer fan counts), shuffler or in order, loyal or explorer, night owl or early bird, skipper or finisher.
- **Your library:** The last page counts your songs, albums, EPs, singles and artists, how long it would take to play them all, and how varied your genres and moods are.
- **What Wrapped counts:** A button at the top of Wrapped explains every source and number it uses.
- **Wrapped fits the screen:** Every Wrapped slide shows all its info at once, no scrolling: a long list shrinks to fit, and on the PC (or a tablet) slides grow a little with the window and sit centred. The summary card uses compact text so all of it shows.
- **Wrapped pictures:** Every song and album shows the cover inside your own copy of it first, read from the file as it is now, so a cover you re-saved shows straight away (if a file's own picture can't be read, as with some M4A/AAC files, the cover the rest of the app shows for it is used); songs you haven't got show a saved cover, then Last.fm's or Deezer's. A played song is matched to your own copy by title and artist even when the names differ a little ("(LBP2 Version)") or the file has moved, so every list (Never skipped, Most skipped too) uses your files' covers; covers from the web are only fetched when you have no copy, and are kept after the first time. A song with no copy here that Last.fm and Deezer have no cover for is looked up the way Most played does (the service, iTunes), else shows its artist's picture, never a blank square.

## The DJ

- **Your own DJ:** Like Spotify's DJ, it plays sets of a few songs at a time, each introduced with a line from the DJ. Sets are built from your listening: what you've had on repeat, what you usually play at this time of day, your favorites, throwbacks to a year you played something to death, songs you loved but haven't heard in months, and new songs like yours from Tidal's and YouTube Music's radios. Songs you keep skipping are left out.
- **Switch it up:** Starts a different kind of set.
- **Next set:** While a DJ set plays, a ⏭ set button on Now playing goes straight to the DJ's next set (its next category), skipping the rest of this one.
- **Never repetitive:** The DJ picks each next set at random from all its kinds; one it just played doesn't come back for about 15 sets (fewer when it can only make a few kinds), and it remembers this across restarts. Its fun facts work the same way: none comes back until about 15 others have been said. It talks like a warm, upbeat radio host, with a different hello for morning, afternoon, evening and late night and several intros for every kind of set, each used before any repeats.
- **Ask for something:** Type an artist, a genre, a decade ("80s") or a vibe ("chill", "workout") and the DJ builds a set for it. A genre plays your own songs tagged with it first (asking for "rock" finds songs tagged Rock or Indie Rock), then Last.fm's.
- **Your genres:** The DJ knows the genre of every song from its file's tags. Now and then it plays a set of a genre you've been playing a lot lately, from your own music, so it works even without Last.fm.
- **From:** Lets the DJ play from everything, from all your songs, or only from a playlist, folder or album you choose. The DJ button on My music and playlists starts it there. On the PC, **Choose a folder…** plays from any folder, even outside your music folders.
- **The DJ talks:** With **Voice on**, the DJ talks between sets, never over a song (the next set's first song waits, loaded and silent, until the DJ is done, then starts from the top): when one set ends it says what's coming next and why ("Let's take it way back to 2020: these are the tracks you were really enjoying"), now and then with a stat, and the next song starts once it's done. **Skip the talk**, Play, Next or Back go straight to the music. On the phone it uses a female Google natural (neural) voice when online, else the best female voice on the phone. On the PC see **Natural DJ voice** below; until it's downloaded it uses Windows' own voice.
- **Did you know?** Now and then between sets the DJ tells you something about your listening: how many hours you've spent on a song coming up, when you first played it, that you've never skipped it (or skip it a lot), your most played artist this month and of all time, the hour you listen most, how many plays it has counted, or a past year's number one song. It shows under the line too.
- **Artists for you:** The DJ works out artists you might like from the ones you play most (all told, with this month counted double) using every play it knows of: Last.fm, your Spotify, Apple Music, Deezer, YouTube Music and Tidal imports, and the app's own. It adds up their similar artists from Last.fm (or Deezer's related artists), leaves out the ones you already play, and now and then plays a set of two new artists with a few of their best known songs ("If you're into A and B, this is right up your alley"). Only when the DJ includes new songs.
- **The DJ's diary:** Every play in the app is kept with its date and time, even with Last.fm on, so the DJ knows when you played what (Throwbacks, Rediscover, This time of day, Did you know). With Last.fm on, the plays it already has aren't counted again, and these plays never count in Most played or your stats.
- **DJ: Your mood:** Sets in the mood you've been in lately, or usually are at this time of day. Ask for a mood ("something chill", "sad songs") and it plays your own songs with that mood first. Now and then it tells you which mood you've been in this month.
- **DJ plays your files:** Every DJ set, from any source (a playlist, a folder, your music, your history, Last.fm or Spotify imports, radios, Mood mix, new songs), uses your own copy of each song you have, shown with your cover and tags from the start, even songs from outside the chosen source.
- **Mood mix:** A DJ set in one mood, a different mood each time it comes round. It mixes your songs in that mood and songs you've streamed in it, and (with new songs on) a couple of new songs the DJ thinks you'll like in the same mood, and introduces it in his own words.
- **Mood facts:** Between sets the DJ can tell you your top mood this week and of all time, a mood you've played more than usual lately, your go-to song in a mood, the time of day you go for a mood, how much of your listening is Hype, or a mood you've drifted away from.
- **Why the voice didn't speak:** If the natural voice can't speak, Voice settings shows why in red (and Windows' voice speaks meanwhile).
- **Natural DJ voice (PC):** **Get the natural voice** downloads Kokoro, a free voice that runs on your PC and sounds like a real person (about 400 MB, once). The DJ speaks in one female voice (Heart); **Hear the DJ** plays a sample. Names it doesn't know (Seesyn, Juice WRLD) are said the way they sound instead of being skipped. The older Piper voice is deleted once Kokoro is in.
- **Talks like a person:** Numbers are said in words, "featuring" instead of "feat.", no symbols read out, contractions, short sentences and the odd "alright".
- **What the DJ knows:** A fold on the DJ screen lists everything the DJ uses to pick its sets and lines.

## Discovery & New releases

- **Discovery playlist:** For any artist, makes a playlist of every song of theirs you don't have yet, album by album. Songs you listen to the end go into a "… discovered" playlist, where you choose to download them or let them go.
- **New releases:** Tap **Refresh…** to check every artist in your music for albums, singles and EPs released in the last week, month or year (from Deezer's catalogue, no account needed). It only checks when you ask. **Select** releases or artists to turn them into a Discovery playlist, checking each song first.

## Tagging & tidying tools

Every scan is in **Settings › Scanner**, in folds by what it's for: **Your files**, **Last.fm & your stats** and **Never show**. Run one when something looks wrong, then forget about it.

On the phone, these tools can change the FLAC files the app downloaded. Android doesn't let the app change other apps' files. On the PC they work on all your music.

- **Fix genre names:** Finds genres spelt more than one way, like “Hip-Hop/Rap” and “Hip-Hop-Rap”, “R&B” and “R and B”, or “Pop” and “pop”, and puts all their songs under one name, written into the files' tags. It starts on the name most of the songs already have; you can pick another of the names, type your own (with your genres suggested as you type), leave a genre alone, or hide it with Never show. On the phone it works on the songs the app downloaded.
- **Tagger…:** Everything about the songs you picked, in one place. **Tags** (with several songs, a field they disagree on is left alone unless you type in it), **Cover** (from Google, Genius or Wikipedia, or your own picture, for one song or the whole album), **Info** (format, quality, size, length and every tag) and **Lyrics** (in the file, found online, or typed in, and saved into the file). On the PC, changing a title can rename the file too.
- **Tagger › Tab:** Puts songs in **Albums**, **EPs** or **Singles** when their tags put them in the wrong one. A release goes to the tab most of its songs are put in, so pick all of an album's songs to move it. **Automatic** goes back to what the tags say. The files aren't changed, and everything else works as usual.
- **Fix albums and artists (PC only):** Fixes albums split up by featured artists ("Quinn XCII & Jeremy Zucker"), albums sitting beside their deluxe, extended or anniversary edition, and singles of songs on an album you have. Songs on a release named as an EP or a single ("Title - EP", "Title - Single") always stay on it, so they never leave the EPs or Singles tab; only their artists may be fixed. Changes go into the file tags; titles are never changed. You check each one first. When it matched two albums by their covers, the other album's cover shows beside the choices, so a wrong match is plain to see. On the PC the files can then be moved to the right folders.
- **Folders to leave alone (PC only):** Folders that no scan ever touches or looks in (Fix albums and artists, Fix genre names, Empty folders, Live in song names, Soundtrack notes in names), and that no songs are moved into. Good for unreleased songs and sessions.
- **Empty folders (PC only):** Finds folders with no songs left in them, only pictures or lyrics files. You see each one's size and contents and choose which to send to the Recycle Bin.
- **Live in song names (PC only):** Finds songs named as live recordings ("Song (Live)", "Song - Live at…") that you'd rather have plain. It removes the live part of the name and keeps the rest. You check each one.
- **Soundtrack notes in names (PC only):** Finds songs whose names carry a note like "(From the Original Motion Picture …)", "[From "Barbie"]" or "- From the Netflix Series …" and takes it off. You choose each one; the ones you leave are not asked about again.
- **Hide things from a scan for good:** Every scan has **Never show** on each thing it lists, so that scan never shows it again, like Duplicates' Not a duplicate (Fix albums and artists, Live in song names, Soundtrack notes in names, Songs without covers, Empty folders). Each scan shows how many you've hidden, with **Show them again** to bring them back; **Reset Never show** in Settings brings back everything hidden in every scan at once. Pressed Never show on the wrong one? **Undo** is right there in its place.
- **Choose yourself:** When none of a scan's choices is right, **Choose yourself…** lets you type your own (an album or an artist in Fix albums and artists, a name in Live in song names and Soundtrack notes), matched against your albums and artists as you type.
- **Songs without covers:** Lists the albums whose songs show no cover anywhere, with **Pick a cover** for each or **Fix them all…** one after the other. Picking a cover also shows pictures of the artist, so an unreleased song with no cover anywhere can wear one of them.
- **Identify by sound…:** Works out what a song is from its sound (AcoustID and MusicBrainz) and offers to write those tags into its file. On the phone it can write them into the app's own downloads; for other songs it shows what they are.
- **Upgrade (Settings › Your music, or Upgrade to FLAC… on songs):** Swaps your songs for Tidal's FLAC where Tidal's is better. MP3s and other lossy files are replaced with FLAC; 16-bit files are replaced only when Tidal has them in 24-bit. Playlists and the queue keep working, and the old files go to the trash so you can get them back. Afterwards it lists the songs it kept because yours were already as good, and you can replace those anyway.
- **Duplicates:** Looks through all your songs (downloads and folders) for ones you have more than once. It shows each copy's name, artist and file, best copy first. Mark each copy **Keep** or **Remove** (the best is kept, the others marked Remove to start with) and remove them all at once, or mark the song **Not a duplicate**.
- **Copies:** See Search & the services: decides what happens when you download a song you already have.

## Phone + PC together

- **Play on / Phone remote:** On the PC, switch on **Phone remote** (Settings › Play on) and it shows a pairing code. On the phone, go to Settings › Play on, choose the PC (it's found on your network automatically) and enter the code. The phone then plays, queues and skips music on the PC, controls its volume and the DJ, and shows what's playing, all from the sofa. Songs stored on the phone stream to the PC over your home network.
- **Sync with phone/PC:** Once the phone is paired with the PC, they sync by themselves every time they connect (right after pairing, when the app opens, when the phone is back on the same Wi-Fi as the PC), and **Sync now** in Settings › Play on syncs any time. The app's own stats go across so both devices show the same: plays, skips, songs heard out and the listening diary (time listened, playlists, outputs, searches), each counted once however many times you sync. Imports and Last.fm aren't sent (import them on each device). **Downloads sync too:** a song you download on one device is downloaded on the other the next time they sync, from the service like any download (tagged, with its cover, in My music); each song is taken once, so one you delete afterwards isn't downloaded again. **Tag changes sync too:** a change you make to a song's tags on one device (Edit tags, the Tagger, Fix titles, album and artist merges, genre merges…) is kept until the next sync, then made to the same song (found by title and artist) on the other, once; a song playing at the time gets it at the following sync. Moods and lyrics go their own way. **Synced playlists (⇄):** Playlists › **New synced playlist**, or **Sync with phone and PC** on any playlist or smart playlist, makes one that's kept exactly the same on the phone and the PC: songs added or taken out on either device are added or taken out on the other at each sync, and deleting it on one deletes it on both. **Stop syncing** leaves each device its own copy. Playlists that aren't synced stay on the device they were made on. It also swaps playlist folders, favorites, pins, plays and skips, moods, Sound presets, headphone corrections and each Bluetooth, USB or car output's EQ, sorts, tabs, the DJ's and your other settings both ways, keeping the newest change of each and losing nothing only one side has. Music folders, the download folder, the queue and device-only settings stay as they are. Tapped on the PC, it waits for the phone to open the app.
- **Songs' own volumes and EQs in Sync:** Off by default, since they're made for each device's headphones; switch it on (on both) to sync them too.
- **Undo last sync:** Each device keeps its data from just before a sync; Undo puts it back.
- **Continue where you left off:** Stop on the PC and open the phone, or the other way round, and it offers the same song at the same spot. This needs the phone paired with the PC.
- **Back up and restore:** Saves your playlists, favorites, plays, queues, settings, equalizer and imported histories in one file, for a new phone or PC or after reinstalling. Your songs and sign-ins aren't included. A phone backup restores on the PC and a PC backup on the phone: that device keeps its own music folders, sound output, device switches, and the volumes and Custom EQs you gave songs (those only go phone to phone or PC to PC, as they fit the headphones you use with each), and the songs that were files on the other device are matched to the same songs on this one (or streamed from your service when it doesn't have them). Restoring keeps what's already there alongside the backup, just in case.
- **Updates:** Settings shows your version and checks for a new release. On the phone, **Update** downloads it and installs it. On the PC it also looks by itself at start and every 12 hours; **Update** downloads the new installer, runs it and closes the app so it can be replaced.
- **Changelog and Features (☰ menu):** **Changelog** lists what each update adds and fixes, the newest at the top, like patch notes. **Features** (this page) describes everything the app does.
- **Problems:** If something went wrong recently, Settings shows it with a **Copy** button so you can send it on.

## Phone only

- **Covers everywhere:** The car's screen, the notification, the lock screen and Windows' media controls show the same cover as the app: one you picked (Tagger or Pick a cover), the download's, or the file's own. Covers are read from FLAC, MP3, M4A (AAC or ALAC), AAC (.aac), WAV and AIFF files, and on the PC from a cover.jpg or folder.jpg next to the song.
- **Android Auto and cars:** Browse Up next, Favorites, Playlists, Recently played and your music on the car's screen, with covers. Play, pause, skip and seek, and use Favorite and Shuffle buttons right on the car's display. With Android Auto on, wired by USB or wireless, the truck is an output of its own (**Chevrolet Colorado · Android Auto**) with its own EQ, starting on the Colorado tuning. It's always in the Equalizer's output list, so you can set it up before you drive.
- **Lock screen player:** When the screen turns on while music plays, the full Now playing appears over the lock screen, with the queue and lyrics. ⌄ takes you on to the normal lock screen. Android asks you once to allow full-screen notifications.
- **Notifications:** The player sits in your notifications and on the lock screen, with shuffle and repeat buttons.
- **Home screen widget:** A small Now playing: the song and cover, with shuffle, previous, play, next and repeat, then the queue (or a big cover), in the cover's colour.
- **Mini player:** Picture-in-picture from the ☰ menu: the cover, the song and its moving bars, floating over other apps.
- **Auto mono:** Mono turns on automatically while your chosen Bluetooth earbuds are connected.

## PC only

- **Casting (both apps):** The Cast button on Now playing sends the music to a Chromecast, Google speaker or TV on your network, starting from where it was. On the PC your songs are sent from the PC itself. Pick **This phone** or **This PC** to bring it back.
- **System tray:** Closing the window keeps the music playing in the tray. Its menu has Play/Pause, Next, Previous, Show Radon Player, Mini player and Quit. When a new song starts while the window is closed, a small notification shows it.
- **Windows media controls:** What's playing shows in Windows' volume flyout and lock screen, and in widgets and wallpapers that follow your music, with play/pause, next, previous and seeking from there.
- **Copy pictures:** On the PC, a Wrapped slide, lyrics card, collage or share card is copied to the clipboard (and saved to Downloads › Radon Player), to paste into any app.
- **First close:** The first time you close the window, a notification says Radon Player is still playing in the tray.
- **Media keys:** Your keyboard's play/pause, next, previous and stop keys control Radon Player from anywhere in Windows.
- **Start with Windows:** Radon Player starts in the tray when you sign in, ready for the media keys and the phone remote.
- **Mini player window:** A small always-on-top window with the cover, the song, a bar to seek with, what plays next, and previous/play/next buttons. The queue button swaps the cover for Up next, with Shuffle, Repeat, Autoplay and your queue.
- **Discord:** Shows what you're playing on your Discord profile, as Spotify does, and clears it when you pause. It works through the Discord app on your PC and sends nothing anywhere else.
- **Discord name:** Discord shows the name of the Discord application the app uses. To see "Listening to Radon Player", make an application called Radon Player at discord.com/developers/applications and paste its Application ID under Discord in Settings. Presence comes back by itself after Discord restarts, and any problem shows in Settings › Problems.
- **Scroll bars:** Every list and screen that scrolls has a scroll bar on its right edge, as in File Explorer: drag it, or click above or below it to move a screen at a time. The scroll wheel works too.
- **Right-click menus:** Right-click anything to get the same menus you'd get with a long-press on the phone. **Esc** goes back.
- **Keyboard shortcuts:** **Space** plays and pauses, **→** or **N** skips, **←** or **P** goes back, **R** changes repeat, **L** likes the song, **F** makes it a favorite, **+** and **−** change the volume, **M** mutes, **W** opens Wrapped, **E** the Equalizer and **/** search. **Ctrl+,** opens Settings, **F9** or **Ctrl+M** the ☰ menu, **Ctrl+F** search and **Ctrl+Q** quits. **Alt+1…9** go to Now playing, Search, My music, Albums, Artists, Playlists, Recently played, Most played and Up next. **S** switches the library you browse (your music, then each service). While picking songs, **Ctrl+A** picks them all, **A** queues them and **Shift+A** plays them next; **Ctrl+click** and **Shift+click** pick songs too. **/** opens the page's own search. **Esc** on Now playing hides the window. In Wrapped, **→**, **Space** or **PageDown** go on, **←** or **PageUp** back, **Home**/**End** to the first or last slide, **S** copies the slide. The one-key shortcuts wait while you're typing in a box.
- **Your own files:** Edit tags, the Tagger, covers and lyrics are written into your own FLAC files too, not only the app's downloads. The Tagger's Tags tab also edits your MP3, M4A, Ogg and other files. WAVs are tagged the way players and Windows both read them (an ID3 tag and a LIST INFO tag inside the file), and studio WAVs with extra chunks of their own (from Pro Tools and the like) are tagged too, keeping those chunks and the sound exactly as they were.
- **One app at a time:** Starting Radon Player again brings the open one to the front. Opening a song file with it plays the song, and commands control the running app, for your own shortcuts and keyboard tools, as the old app's: `play SONG` (a search, a link or a file), `next SONG`, `queue SONG`, `play`, `pause`, `play-pause`, `skip`, `prev`, `lyrics [genius|synced]`, `search WORDS`, `show TAB`, `accounts`, `wrapped [YEAR|month|week|all]`, `source youtube|tidal|apple|spotify|mine`, `tag [all]`, `status`, `hide`, `quit`, `help`, and `--tidal-login`, `--lastfm-login`, `--spotify-login`.
- **Deleting your own songs:** **Delete…** works on your own files too, and they go to the Recycle Bin, as on the old PC app.
- **Downloads anywhere:** Settings › Downloads › Change can pick any folder on the PC with **Another folder…**.
- **PC-only sound and tools:** Empty folders, and moving fixed songs into the right folders.
- **Signing in on the PC:** Spotify and Last.fm sign in by themselves: their page opens in your browser, you agree there, and the app notices on its own. Tidal's page ends on an "Oops" address that you copy back into the app, as on the old PC app.
